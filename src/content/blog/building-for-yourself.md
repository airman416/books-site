The best products often start as solutions to personal problems. Here's why that matters.

Every founder hears the advice: "Build something people want." It's good advice. But it misses something important.

Build something you want first. Then figure out if other people want it too.

## Why Personal Problems Matter

When you're solving your own problem, you know things other people can't tell you:

- What the real pain point is (not what people say it is)
- What solutions you've already tried
- What "good enough" actually looks like
- When you'd actually use it versus when you think you would

This knowledge is the difference between building something useful and building something that sounds useful.

## The Risk

The obvious risk: your problems might be unique. What works for you might not work for anyone else.

This is a real risk. But it's better than the alternative: building something for "everyone" that works for no one.

## How to Test It

If you're building for yourself, here's how to know if others want it too:

1. Build the smallest version that solves your problem
2. Use it yourself for a month
3. Then show it to five people with similar problems
4. Watch what they do, not what they say

If they ask where to sign up, you might have something. If they say "interesting idea," you probably don't.

## What I'm Building

The tool I'm working on now started because I was drowning in information. Too many articles saved, too many notes scattered, too many "I should read this" tabs open.

I built something simple to organize my thinking. It worked for me. Then I showed it to other founders, and they had the same problem.

Now I'm turning it into a product. But I'm not adding features other people request unless I'd use them myself.

## The Principle

Build for yourself first. Not exclusively. But first.

If you wouldn't use it, why would anyone else?
