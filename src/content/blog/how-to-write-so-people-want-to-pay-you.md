> If people cannot write well, they cannot think well; and if they cannot think well, others will do their thinking for them.” - George Orwell

In the age of information overload, writing effectively could be the difference between being heard and being buried.

Writing isn’t just pen on paper or hands on keyboard—it is akin to speech, which is akin to thought.

The better you can write, the clearer you can think, and the clearer you can speak.

There are some people that get heard every time they speak—Barack Obama, Simon Sinek, MLK Jr., Tony Robbins, Steve Jobs, Abraham Lincoln. 

These are people that can get complex ideas across without sounding like a smart-ass and in a comprehensible manner.

They weren’t necessarily born that way. They’re not superhuman in that everything that comes out of their mouth is gold.

Beneath the hood, while they say different things, they say it in the same way.

Your favorite movies, your favorite songs—they’re different, but they follow the same structure. 

In fact, structure is MAGIC. It makes complex ideas palatable to our constantly distracted minds. 

“Structure” is not a set of arbitrary rules that a professor at Harvard invented in the year 1950. It isn’t even rules. It is patterns. The reason structure has stayed so long is not because people have chosen to use them. It is because they work.

In the many years of human content—books, plays, speeches, stories, songs—there are only a few that are remembered. And the ones that are remembered get imitated.

Well, why do these pieces of content get remembered? 

It is because they make us _feel something_. 

It turns out that there are only a few ways for us to get to _feel something_. 

So in fact, structure is the subconscious language that speaks universally to the human heart. 

Anyone can learn to command this language—whether it is to:

  1. Feel heard

  2. Not stumble over your words

  3. Command presence

  4. Articulate complex but valuable ideas in a way that people are drawn to it




After studying writers like Dan Koe, Paul Graham, Jordan Peterson, and writing over 100,000 words across Quora and my blog (including draft content), here’s what I’ve found that WORKS.

* * *

### Screw grammar. Get your idea across.

Write in a way that’d make your English teacher from high-school have an aneurism. 

In the real world, nobody cares about whether you used the right punctuation, grammar, or whether you know what a semicolon does. As long as it’s legible, fun to read, and valuable.

Give people a chance to read the writing you worked so hard on. Here are a few things I’ve noticed most great writers. It applies primarily to informal essays, and content meant to drive action—copywriting.

  * Short sentences

  * Whitespace

  * EMPHASIS—through capitalization or italics

  * Rhetorical questions

  * Simple words no jargon (buzzwords turn people off, be down-to-earth)

  * Use ellipses…

  * Extenddddd your words for emphasis 

  * Don’t be afraid to use passive voice

  * Create concise, quotable ideas

  * Cut ~~out~~ ~~as many~~ useless words ~~as possible~~

  * Make it airy, spaced out and easy to read! (Have you ever opened a book, see giant paragraphs and immediately start skimming?)




Make your writing appealing to read and reward your reader by letting them make vertical progress. 

Focus on conversational rather than grammatically correct. You’re not getting any points from your teacher, your reader, or your customer for following grammar.

In fact, never write above a sixth-grade level. Google what an F-K score is (trust me).

## The Two Frameworks for Persuasive Writing 

There are two meta-frameworks that I’ve found that encapsulate all the other structures that great writers use to get their ideas across.

Can you guess which one I’m using right now?

###  **1\. Problem-Centric Framework**

 **“You’re in pain and don’t realize how bad it is.”**

#### Problem - Amplify - Solution

  1. Identify the problem

  2. Intensify the pain/frustration, 

  3. Deliver your solution. 




Works best when your audience already knows they have a problem but hasn’t acted yet.

[Dan Koe PAS Example #1](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/the-social-matrix-how-the-1-elites)

* * *

####  **QUEST - Qualify, Understand, Educate, Stimulate, Transition**

  1. Qualify your reader (make them feel it’s for them)

  2. Show you Understand their situation

  3. Educate on why this matters

  4. Stimulate desire for change

  5. Transition them to action (CTA)




The Q and U are most important here. 

Write for the same person in all your writing. Zero in on a person that encapsulates the entire demographic.

The best feeling is when you read something and feel it was written just for you. Let people feel that through your writing.

Good examples of this:

[Dan Koe QUEST Example #1 ](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/how-to-use-ai-better-than-99-of-people)

* * *

### 2\. Outcome-Centric Framework

 **“Something amazing exists, and you don’t have it yet.”**

####  **AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)**

  1. Hook their Attention (surprising stat, bold claim, relatable scenario)

  2. Deepen Interest by connecting to their situation, showing you understand them

  3. Make them Desire the outcome. Paint the after-state, show social proof, create FOMO

  4. Clear Action step to make change (including CTA)




This works best for copywriting. You’re not giving away the sauce here, you want them to take the desired action in the CTA.

[Famous Coat of Arms Letter](https://swiped.co/file/coat-of-arms-letter-by-gary-halbert/)

[Some of the best ads of all time](https://marketingsecrets.com/blog/best-ads-of-all-time)

[Famous Dollar Bill ad](https://swiped.co/file/famous-dollar-letter-by-gary-halbert/) (SO GOOD).

* * *

####  **BAB - Before, After, Bridge**

  1. Paint the current state (before)

  2. Show the transformed future (after)

  3. Then explain how to get there (bridge). 




Highly effective for transformation-focused content because people viscerally feel the gap between where they are and where they want to be.

[Dan Koe BAB Example #1](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/the-death-of-the-9-5-job-why-everyone)

[Dan Koe BAB Example #2](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/a-dopamine-detox-to-reset-your-life)

### How to use these frameworks

Every framework I’ve described here creates **gap tension** :

  * Current painful state ↔ Desired better state

  * What they know ↔ What they need to know

  * Where they are ↔ Where they want to be

## Story Frameworks

The above frameworks are not just ways to throw information at your audience. To be most effective, try to write a story.

All stories you read will follow one of the following frameworks, developed and retold over centuries.

### 1\. Underdog/Rags to Riches

 **“Started in a garage with $200. Now we’re the #3 coffee roaster in America.”**

Sounds kind of like BAB doesn’t it? 

It’s not a coincidence. 

Relate with the audience who might be in the “Before” stage, show the result (After), and show how to get there (Bridge).

* * *

###  **2\. David vs. Goliath/Overcoming the Monster**

**“Big Tech was selling your data. We built the first truly private messenger.”**

This one sounds like PAS, just missing the A (Amplify) stage.

* * *

### 3\. Mission/Quest

 **“Making a backpack tough enough to survive all four years of college without falling apart.”**

I wish this one fit the QUEST framework. I really do. But it’s more like AIDA.

* * *

### 4\. Transformation 

**“Sarah went from 3 hours of sleep to running marathons. Her secret? Our sleep protocol.”**

This also sounds like BAB.

* * *

### 5\. Discovery/Voyage

 **“Spent 2 years in Japanese kitchens. Brought back the knife that changed Western cooking.”**

This one sounds like AIDA.

* * *

### 6\. Comedy

 **“Our CEO wore the prototype backwards on live TV. It became our best-selling design.”**

Outlier.

* * *

### 7\. Tragedy

 **“Lost my brother to distracted driving. Built the app that’s now required in 12 school districts.”**

PAS/QUEST.

## What do I do with this information?

Ok, so now you have a bunch of frameworks in your tool belt. Having the tools is one thing—using them is another. Here’s how to actually pick where to use each framework: it all starts with **audience** , **purpose** , and **context**.

While reading this, keep in mind some piece of writing you have in progress, or something you want to write in the future.

### Audience

Write for the same person in all your writing. Zero in on a SINGLE person that encapsulates the entire demographic. This lets you have consistent messaging throughout your writing—and all your pieces of writing. 

Don’t you feel great when you read something and feel it was written just for you?

Let people feel that through your writing.

Once you have a single person in mind, write out the following:

  1. What is their problem? How bad is it? What fixes it? (PAS)

  2. What might grab their attention? Why should they care? What do they want? What do you want them to do? (AIDA)

  3. Who is this for? What do they struggle with? What do they need to know? What do they want? What should they do? (QUEST)

  4. Where are they now? Where could they be? How do they get there? (BAB)




Once you’re done with this, judge which one is the most compelling. 

If you’re struggling to decide, continue…

* * *

### Purpose and Context

Why are YOU writing? Why are THEY reading?

  1. Selling something or driving action? Do you think the reader will benefit? (AIDA)

  2. Reader already knows they have a problem (but maybe don’t know how bad, or don’t have a solution). Are you trying to demonstrate the value of your information? (PAS)

  3. Are you trying to build an audience? Audience is really specific and you can relate to them? (QUEST)

  4. Do you have a great transformation you can show off? Do you think the method will benefit the reader? (BAB)




* * *

### Now what?

Don’t try to memorize these frameworks. Internalize them. It takes time for that to happen. In the meantime, bookmark this post and come back to it when you’re actually writing something.

In the meantime, pick something you’ve already written. Grab a notebook or open your Notes app. Answer the 4 questions in the **Audience** section. 

Which set of answers is the most compelling?

Outline the framework you think works best. Don’t write like a brain dump, this isn’t free-writing! Free-writing has its time and place, and I love it for getting ideas out. But this is writing to get people to _feel_ something. 

Get the first draft out as fast as you can and let it marinate. 

The good ideas come when you’re walking down the street later.

Then start writing again.

Shonda Rhimes says she walks through five miles of “writing crap and doodling and trying to have an idea and surfing the internet and hoping like hell not to get so distracted that you give up” before she gets to the “good stuff.” And she’s written 11 SEASONS of Greys’s Anatomy. She’s a powerhouse.

There’s no such thing as writer’s block, there’s just laziness. If you keep dumping out content, at least some percent of that is going to be good. Not all of it has to be good. 

Either way, if you think all your writing is good, you’re in for a big surprise.

 **Bonus tip: write right after you wake up because you censor yourself less.**

Once you’re done writing, give your brain a moment to catch up. In the meantime, consume a wide variety of content across many disciplines. Work on other projects and have conversations with people that are different from you.

This helps you improve upon the audience and context portion of your writing, and lets you incorporate quotes and examples that make you seem like a well-read, informed individual.

To recap:

  1. Answer the questions in the **Audience, Purpose, Context** section.

  2. Decide which framework is the most compelling.

  3. Get a first draft following the outline out. Don’t censor yourself at this stage.

  4. Take a break. Consume other high-quality writing.

  5. Come back and edit further.

### Swipe File

While you’re not writing, create a **swipe file.**

I have a swipe file for absolutely everything. 

Tweets, TikToks, blog posts, landing pages, YouTube videos, quotes, phrases, aphorisms—anything that stood out to me and that my brand could relate to.

This is because my brand is simply the set of 1. ideas and 2. delivery (ranging from writing frameworks to mediums like text, video, audio, short-form, long-form) that I relate to and am re-packaging.

It is crucial that ideas are repeated in various delivery formats. Don’t try to reinvent the wheel! That is the whole purpose of the article you are currently reading.

A strong swipe file that becomes incorporated—consciously or subconsciously into your brand is indispensable. If you don’t have one already, START TODAY.

Anything works, Notes app, Google Docs—I actually use Trello since I can organize by topic.

Here’s some other stuff that can go in your swipe file.

Personal stories, organized by topic, written in one of the storytelling frameworks from above. You can reuse the same story and write it in multiple different frameworks.

Metaphors. Anything you see being repeated and that people seem to relate to.

Statistics. Anything that stands out, organized by topic.

Quotes. Great quotes could launch a whole article by themselves. Don’t go crazy though, and once again organize by topic because a lot of quotes repeat themselves in different ways. Pick and choose the most punchy ones.

* * *

### Be candid.

Finally, when writing your solution or your CTA, be HONEST.

Please don’t make exaggerated claims (buy my course and you will become a billionaire!) because people can tell. On the other hand, people WILL register and appreciate honesty.

The risk of lying far outweighs the reward. If the product or idea is good you won’t have to lie.

Honesty also helps you win customers over the competitor. You allow customers to see that you care about them. 

You create confidence for the customer to pick you, between two identical goods.
