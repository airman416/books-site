Most productivity systems don't fail because they're bad. They fail because they ignore how humans actually work.

I spent a year testing different systems with real teams. The results were clear: the system doesn't matter nearly as much as we think.

## The Pattern

Every productivity system follows the same arc:

1. Initial excitement and setup
2. A few weeks of perfect execution
3. Slow drift away from the system
4. Guilt about not using the system
5. Looking for a new system

The problem isn't the system. It's the assumption that you need a system at all.

## What Actually Works

After watching dozens of people try different approaches, three things showed up in every successful case:

**Clear outcomes**: People knew what success looked like. Not "be more productive." Specific results they could measure.

**Minimal overhead**: The system took less than 5 minutes a day to maintain. Any more, and it became another task to manage.

**Flexibility**: The system adapted when life changed. Rigid systems break. Flexible ones bend.

## The Real Insight

Productivity isn't about doing more things. It's about doing the right things and ignoring everything else.

The best "system" most people need:
- Write down the three most important things today
- Do those first
- Everything else is optional

That's it.

## Why This Is Hard

The problem with simple systems is they feel too easy. We want complexity because it makes us feel like we're doing something important.

But complexity is usually just procrastination wearing a productivity costume.

## What I Do Now

I don't use a productivity system anymore. I use two rules:

1. If it's important, do it first thing
2. If it's not important, don't do it

Most people's productivity problems are priority problems in disguise.
