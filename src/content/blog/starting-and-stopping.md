Most advice tells you to never quit. That's wrong. Knowing when to stop is just as important as knowing when to start.

I've started three companies. I shut down two of them myself. The third one I should have stopped earlier than I did.

## The Problem with "Never Give Up"

The persistence narrative sounds good. It makes for inspiring stories. But it ignores a simple truth: your time and energy are finite. Spending them on the wrong thing is the real failure.

Every hour you spend on something that isn't working is an hour you can't spend on something that might.

## What I Learned

After shutting down my second company, I made a list of signals that something isn't working:

- You're solving problems you created, not problems customers have
- Growth requires more effort each month, not less
- You dread working on it
- You keep telling yourself "just one more month"

If three of these are true, it's time to stop. Not pivot. Stop.

## Moving On

Stopping isn't failure. It's clearing space for the next thing. The best decision I made last year was shutting down a project I'd worked on for eight months.

Two months later, I started something better. I wouldn't have seen the opportunity if I'd still been grinding on the old thing.

## The Real Question

Don't ask "Should I quit?" Ask "If I started today, would I start this?"

If the answer is no, you have your answer.
